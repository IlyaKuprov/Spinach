# kernel/utilities/floquet_magnus.m

Computes a finite-harmonic high-frequency expansion without time integration, returning both an effective matrix generator and a periodic kick. It implements the Fourier recurrence in Appendix A of Bock, Ernst, and Uhrig, DOI 10.5194/mr-2026-10.

Use `[F,K]=floquet_magnus(H,omega,order,t0,theory)`. The odd-length row cell array `H` contains square double matrices in the order `H_-N,...,H_0,...,H_N`, with the convention `H(t)=sum H_n*exp(1i*n*omega*t)`. The scalar controls `omega`, `order`, and `t0` are real doubles; frequencies are angular frequencies and `t0` is in seconds. Sparse input is supported. The effective inverse-frequency power `order` ranges from zero to three: zero returns the mean generator, while three corresponds to the paper's fourth order, not a correction beyond Spinach's existing third inverse-frequency power.

The `van_vleck` gauge has zero-mean kick coefficients. The `aht` gauge makes the kick vanish at `t0` at each retained order. The returned `K` contains harmonics from `-(order+1)*N` to `(order+1)*N`, with one extra inverse-frequency power relative to `F` so that intra-cycle evolution can be reconstructed. Evaluate its Fourier series at both endpoints and form `expm(-1i*K(t))*expm(-1i*F*(t-t0))*expm(1i*K(t0))`. The endpoint kicks are essential: matching quasienergies does not establish the correct propagator or complex signal. Different gauges agree asymptotically, not exactly after truncation.

No Hamiltonian symmetrisation, state normalisation, or positivity repair is performed. Hermitian time-dependent input requires `H_-n=H_n'`. General matrix generators are accepted algebraically, but that does not establish complete positivity or a relaxation model. Convergence and long-time accuracy must be checked for the intended drive and observation interval.

This does not replace `average`, the MAS `floquet` context, or `vvpert`. In particular, `drifts` can obtain native Fokker-Planck reference generators from `singlerot`; those enlarged matrices include the spatial derivative and are not themselves temporal Fourier coefficients. Use `rotor_stack` to obtain physical rotor-phase Hamiltonians for Fourier transformation. The native integration regression verifies their reconstruction against the phase blocks of the independently generated Fokker-Planck operator, then compares complete complex signals. Its native transport convention requires `omega=-2*pi*parameters.rate` for the phase series used in that test.
