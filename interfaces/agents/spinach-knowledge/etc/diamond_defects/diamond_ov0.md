# etc/diamond_defects/diamond_ov0.m

- Signature: [sys,inter]=diamond_ov0(parameters)

## Purpose

Constructs a spin system for the neutral oxygen-vacancy centre OV0 (WAR5) in diamond. Magnetic parameters are taken from Tables 9-2 and 9-3 of B. L. Cann, *Magnetic Resonance Studies of Point Defects in Diamond*, PhD thesis, University of Warwick (2009), https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/3125. The zero-field splitting and assignment to OV0 at 4 K are supported by S. Mukherjee et al., *Phys. Rev. B* **114**, 074105 (2026), https://doi.org/10.1103/3dcd-mkcq.

## Physical / mathematical content

The centre has S = 1 and C3v symmetry. The electron g and zero-field-splitting tensors are axial about the trigonal axis; the rhombicity is zero within experimental error. Oxygen is omitted because the predominant natural isotope, 16O, has no nuclear spin. The model includes one representative 13C nucleus for each of four shells labelled a, g, l, and c. The thesis assigns 3, 6, 3, and 3 symmetry-equivalent carbon atoms to these shells, respectively.

## Numerical / algorithmic content

The electron g principal values are 2.0025, 2.0025, and 2.0029, with D = 2888 MHz. The source's four 13C hyperfine tensors are constructed from their principal values and axes, symmetrised, and rotated into the requested orientation. For shells a and g, the symmetry-relaxed fit is used, which the thesis reports as the better of the two fits. A single representative nucleus per shell is returned; other members of a shell are generated by C3v operations about the trigonal axis.

## Parameters / inputs

- parameters.orientation: '111', '110', or '100'; the corresponding crystal-plane normal is aligned with the magnetic field.

## Outputs

- sys: Spinach system specification structure.
- inter: Spinach interaction specification structure.

## Implementation structure

The function validates the orientation, creates the E3-plus-four-13C isotope and label lists, then builds and rotates the electron and carbon tensors. It leaves the four symmetry-related carbon sites represented once each rather than expanding all equivalent atoms.
