- Signature: `spin_phonon_avoided_crossing()`
- Source: [MATLAB example](https://github.com/IlyaKuprov/Spinach/blob/main/examples/quantum_tech/spin_phonon_avoided_crossing.m)

## Model

The example diagonalises the one-excitation sector of an electron–phonon Jaynes–Cummings exchange model while sweeping the spin–phonon detuning. Its particle list `{'E','V3'}` is one multiplicity-2 electron spin and a phonon mode truncated to three levels. Here `V3` is the virtual phonon-mode notation, not a vanadium or defect isotope. The source sets the magnet field to zero, the phonon rotating-frame frequency to zero, and the exchange coefficient to `4e6` (4 MHz); it converts this to angular coupling `g=2*pi*4e6`.

The model uses the `zeeman-hilb` basis without a basis approximation and retains spin–mode exchange under the `cavity` assumption. The detuning is swept from −20 to +20 MHz at 121 points. The two basis states are the spin-excited/phonon-vacuum state and the spin-ground/one-phonon state; for each detuning the corresponding 2-by-2 Hamiltonian is diagonalised. The calculated minimum splitting is checked against `2g`, giving an 8 MHz gap in frequency units.

## What the plot represents

The plotted curves are the two dressed eigenenergies of that restricted one-quantum Hamiltonian, reported in MHz against detuning in MHz. They show the avoided crossing generated by the modelled exchange term; they are not a time trace, a measured phonon spectrum, or an EPR absorption spectrum. No drive, relaxation, or dissipation is specified.

## Defect and selection limits

The generic `E` particle is spin one-half. Although the model is useful for illustrating spin–phonon hybridisation, it does not encode a particular defect's nuclear isotope, higher-spin electronic structure, zero-field splitting, or EPR field/frequency selection. No experimental spectrum or device fidelity is inferred from the eigenvalue plot. The source labels its calculation time as seconds; that is not a benchmark or convergence result.
