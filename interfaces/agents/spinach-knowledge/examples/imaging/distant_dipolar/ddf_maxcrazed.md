# MAXCRAZED with two proton resonances

[Chen, Branca and Warren (2012), Fig. 1(c) and Table I](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.4721637) compare X-CRAZED with XY- and XZ-MAXCRAZED for equal starting volumes of water and acetone. The X correlation lobes have a 1:2 area ratio. A balanced second pair at ten times the first X gradient winds and unwinds the transverse magnetisation along Y or Z. These two encodings have identical diffusion weighting under isotropic diffusion; the Fourier angular factor of their transverse magnetisation does not. In XZ, the transverse dipolar torque opposes the longitudinal self-field torque, suppressing homomolecular peaks. Water and acetone share the proton gyromagnetic ratio but are chemically distinct frames, so only their longitudinal dipolar fields act across frames.

The two-dimensional acquisition retains the selected double-quantum indirect branch. The frequency pairs (Hz) are (3000, 1500), (2130, 1500), (2130, 630), and (1260, 630), in Table I order. Each predicted magnitude is on the same scale, set by the strongest of the four X-CRAZED peaks. The observed intensities are those in Table I's absolute columns, not the parenthesised intensities with separately normalised water peaks. Hetero/homo divides the sum of the two mixed peaks by the sum of the two self peaks; the reported measured ratios come from the paper, not from recalculation on rounded intensities.

The example uses the 630 and 1500 Hz offsets and 1.5 ms rectangular lobe duration from the related CRAZED data in Fig. 3, and the 360 MHz field described for the GRACE experiment. The 60 ms delay separates the two strong lobes; X-CRAZED has the same elapsed time with both strong lobes set to zero. The absolute correlation gradient, sample temperature and mixture diffusivities were not independently specified for Table I. The model uses a 1 mm X grating pitch, Curie magnetisations at 298.15 K from the solvent proton number densities, and nominal 2 × 10⁻⁹ m²/s diffusion for each species. It uses a periodic field multiplier with zero uniform mode, reflecting diffusion on the computational cell, a hard-pulse approximation (10 µs rectangular pulses), and a short, unapodised acquisition with bin-centred frequencies. It omits relaxation, radiation damping, the experimental tube geometry, and the unspecified spectral processing. Neither parameters nor peak intensities are fitted, and residuals are differences on the common X scale, not a statistical goodness of fit.

The nominal prediction suppresses both self peaks upon changing XY to XZ and retains both mixed peaks. The hetero/homo ratio is about 0.64 for XY and 9.71 for XZ, versus measured values of 0.68 and 2.65. The model therefore distinguishes the orientations but overpredicts the XZ ratio and predicts less XY attenuation than Table I reports. The small remaining XZ self peaks make the ratio sensitive to integration error: halving the maximum substep from 0.5 to 0.25 ms at common indirect times changes it from 9.71 to 11.02 without changing the qualitative ordering. The delay sweep in Fig. 5 is not used for calibration.
