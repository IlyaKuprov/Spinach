# Finite-gradient CRAZED in doped water

`ddf_barros_water(periods,points_per_wave,max_step,workers)` compares the echo build-up from the production `ddf_sequence` with the digitised open circles in Fig. 2 of [Barros et al.](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.3116107). The two orientations are parallel and perpendicular to the main field, each at four gradient strengths. The water is represented by one proton resonance in its rotating frame. The measured (T_1), (T_2), and diffusivity set the relaxation and diffusion, and the reported dipolar time sets the equilibrium magnetisation. The four nominal gradient amplitudes identify the measured series. The simulation uses their reported inverse diffusion weights (6.58, 1.64, 0.73, 0.41) to calculate the wave number and hence the rectangular lobe amplitude. Absolute echo amplitudes use an independent gradient-free 90° receiver reference; no gain or time offset is fitted to Fig. 2.

The waveforms implement Fig. 1b with both finite rectangular gradient lobes at the same amplitude, the second twice as long, and acquisition at the echo centre. The refocusing pulse is centred between the two equal intervals labelled τ, following the fixed (2t_1) delay after the second gradient. The first pulse phase advances by 90° per transient, with signed receiver weights ([1,-1,1,-1]/4) to select coherence order two. Mixing and refocusing phases remain fixed. The article does not specify RF pulse widths or the exact cycle phases; the example uses 10 μs rectangular hard pulses and supplies an ideal four-transient selection. It omits hardware gradient ramps, background gradients, radiation damping, and RF/receive inhomogeneity.

A practical reduced invocation is `ddf_barros_water(4,16,0.002,8)`. Here four complete grating periods are resolved with 16 voxels per wavelength, diffusion is periodic, and spatially uniform magnetisation sees the zero spherical distant field. Grid and time steps are explicit; the applied-gradient ramp itself is not periodic during a lobe, so repeat with more periods to measure the effect of diffusion across the seam. `periods=0` instead configures the physical 8 mm sphere on a Cartesian grid with reflecting diffusion at the voxelised boundary and isolated magnetostatics. Resolving the shortest wavelength (58.4 μm) at 16 voxels per wavelength needs about 2,192 voxels along each axis, before the approximately eightfold padding for the isolated FFT. The full geometry is not the reduced numerical benchmark.

`barros_fig2.csv` contains numerical coordinates extracted from the experimental symbols, not raw scanner data or a copy of the figure. Its two picks and uncertainty columns quantify digitisation differences, not experimental uncertainty. The example reports signed pointwise residuals, RMSE for each curve, and peak height and sampled peak time differences. The solid simulations and the dashed Eq. (1) reference are compared to the same observed points without a fitted gain. Model peaks for the summary are sampled at the digitised times using shape-preserving interpolation of the calculated curves.

At four periods, 16 voxels per period, and a 2 ms maximum step, the unfitted pooled RMSE against 665 digitised points is 0.00160 M₀ (the first-order approximation gives 0.00465 M₀). The peak heights fall monotonically with increasing gradient strength in both orientations. In the perpendicular 0.05 T/m case the peak is nevertheless 0.00296 M₀ too high. Halving the integration step, increasing to 24 voxels per period, and doubling the cell length individually changed predicted amplitudes by at most 0.00095, 0.00039, and 0.00024 M₀, respectively. These are numerical sensitivities, not experimental errors.

The third output, `traces`, retains the uncombined complex signal for every shot, its curve-specific complex reference, and the signed receiver weights. Combine shots before taking the magnitude. The peak table also reports digitisation-only bounds on observed peak amplitude and time: any sampled point whose upper amplitude reaches the greatest lower amplitude is a possible peak. The spread under separate numerical refinements must be reported independently. Fig. 2 does not supply numerical preparation, magnetisation, relaxation, or diffusion uncertainties, so these bounds must not be interpreted as a statistical goodness of fit.

The isolated-sphere calculation processes one curve at a time to avoid holding several full spatial models concurrently. At 16 voxels per wavelength, the strongest encoding requires 2,192 cells per side and a 4,383-cubed isolated convolution. The eight live real double arrays in the solid-angle kernel construction alone require about 5.39 TB, excluding the complex FFT workspace, diffusion, and propagation. The bulk result above does not pass the corresponding isolated-sphere gate; a wavelength-resolved run and independent real-space and time refinements remain necessary.
