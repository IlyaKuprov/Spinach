# Solvent-referenced HOMOGENIZED spectroscopy

`ddf_homogenized()` compares conventional and two-dimensional spectra of a weakly coupled solute doublet with a static 200 Hz ramp across a 10 mm receiver. The direct SQ coordinate retains common offsets, whereas the strong zero-quantum branch is at minus the solute–solvent difference in native FFT coordinates. Its mirror partner is weaker by the squared tangent of half the mixing angle. The example is based on [Vathyam, Lee and Warren (1996)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.272.5258.92); it does not fit their measured spectra.

The calculation uses the analytic dilute-reporter limit of a bulk longitudinal solvent grating. With grating wave number k along x, the ramp adds wave number h in the yz plane at the magic angle to z. The cavity-corrected longitudinal dipolar multiplier becomes k²/[3(k²+h²)]. At increasing correlation distance π/k the indirect signal therefore decays faster. Widths are measured on an isolated component, not across overlapping peaks. Inhomogeneity is integrated over the receive aperture; the field source is unbounded, not truncated at the receiver faces. The example is a spectral benchmark, not a simulation of reciprocal feedback or a validation of the general pulse integrator.
