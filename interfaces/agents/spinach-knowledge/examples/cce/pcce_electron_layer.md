# Partitioned Hahn echo in a two-dimensional electron lattice

`[answer,diagnostics]=pcce_electron_layer()` reconstructs the blue pCCE(2,1), green internally averaged pCCE(2,2), orange normally averaged pCCE(2,2), red pCCE(2,4), and purple pCCE(2,5) series in [Schätzle et al., supplementary Fig. S4(a)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.16169). The five result columns are the normalised NV magnetisations in that order. Diagnostics supplies total echo times in seconds, lattice sites in nm, and the partitions (`pairs`, `groups_four`, and `groups_five`).

The two NV branches have magnetic quantum numbers 0 and −1. All 20 bath electrons interact by secular dipolar couplings. Within each cluster both echo paths are propagated and traced at infinite temperature; outside it, a common random longitudinal spin configuration supplies the static field. For partition sizes one, four, and five, the example averages after forming the second-order cluster product; for dimers, it evaluates both averaging orders. The distinction matters when a cluster response appears in a denominator.

The supplement specifies a planar lattice with 20 nm nearest-neighbour spacing, but not the precise sites, partition, or exterior spin draws. The example uses the 20 nearest square-lattice sites around the NV, a minimum-total-squared-distance pairing, and 100 reproducible exterior configurations. For sizes four and five, it uses equal-size constrained k-means (`random_state=0`, as in the authors' code) on those same sites, giving largest clusters of eight and ten spins. The partition lists are stored explicitly so that MATLAB needs no Python dependency. These are reconstruction choices, not original simulation inputs. The exact 20-spin benchmark is not calculated.

Both conditional Hamiltonians conserve total bath magnetic quantum number, so the trace is a sum over independent sectors, weighted by their Hilbert-space dimensions. The largest sector of a 10-spin cluster has 252 states rather than 1024. In the eigenbasis of one branch, its propagator is diagonal, and the trace reduces to a cosine-weighted sum of the squared magnitudes of the other branch's propagator elements. This gives the full infinite-temperature cluster trace, without truncating sectors or using random pure states.

Comparison with identifiable published marker centres gives RMS magnetisation differences of 0.025 (10 blue markers) and 0.041 (16 green markers). The normally averaged dimer result is not a quantitative match: it first exceeds unity at 200 μs on this grid, while the published orange series is still below 0.15 there. The higher-order reconstructions have RMS differences of 0.035 (12 red markers, 97–245 μs) and 0.025 (13 purple markers, 59–244 μs). The plotted ordinate is limited to the published range; the returned values are not clipped.
