# Range-aware evaluation of a fixed POD family

`pod_evolve(model,coefficients,timestep,nsteps)` returns the initial complex observable and a uniform trajectory of the prepared affine model. Inputs and integer counts are double; nsteps is nonnegative and below flintmax. Terms use L=H+1i*R+1i*K in rad/s, time is in seconds, and the sign is exp(-1i*L*t).

Time, coefficients and active terms are decomposed into bounded components and binary exponents. Assembly uses one common exponent frame and restores only the summed generator, preserving finite cancellation without trial multiplication orders or fallback chains. Lost nonzero components and an unrepresentable final generator are explicit errors. Zero coefficients are excluded. Sparse terms remain sparse through the exponential-action callback.

Initial coordinates and the detector are normalised separately. An estimated real Rayleigh quotient supplies a scalar amplitude shift; it is not an invariance test. Native exponential actions update binary-normalised working coordinates. Physical amplitude is carried separately; only the detected Hermitian overlap is restored. A finite observable can therefore remain supported beyond the physical state's double range. A shift whose exponent is outside exact integer range is not used. Unsupported working-array dynamic range, actions that entirely underflow to zero, unscalarised action norms above flintmax, inexactly representable accumulated exponents and nonfinite final observables are explicit errors. Physical signal underflow follows double arithmetic and can round to zero. A separate analytical bound certifies entirely underflowed future scalar signals from the largest finite complex initial signal; it does not test a computed working action. This is not arbitrary-precision or componentwise-error certification for ill-conditioned dynamics.

Zero-step, zero-duration, all-zero coefficients, zero initial state/detector and exact common-side structural stationary support return constant traces after input validation. A state-side shortcut must hold for every active term, or a detector-side shortcut must hold for every active term; different sides do not combine. The assembled support is also checked. Computed actions are never used to infer zeros because they can underflow. Cancellation-based nullspaces otherwise use the general exponential action; zero initial overlap does not imply a constant signal.

MATLAB expmv implements the Al-Mohy–Higham exponential-action method, DOI:10.1137/100788860. The documented operator callback keeps the explicit scalar shift as the only shift. Actions are streamed in blocks no wider than the reduced rank; a Hermitian-part Gershgorin growth bound limits their range. The full propagator and full trajectory are never formed. Scalar models use their closed-form scalar exponential. The equivalent forward or adjoint action with the lower initial coordinate norm-growth rate is selected; the adjoint result is conjugated to retain the original observable. Native norm estimates preserve caller RNG on acceptance and error. No invariant subspace is inferred from a small computed action. This evaluator does not train, retune rank, change a physical spin_system, infer a metric, subtract references or process spectra. No positivity, trace, stability or observable-error guarantee follows from snapshot energy or orthogonality.

Hermitian detection accumulates real component products by their binary exponents before restoring signal units. Structural directed reachability retains exactly the coordinates on paths from the state to the detector, excluding unrelated unstable blocks. Normalized matrix products explicitly reject unsupported component underflow.

The matrix-product range contract conservatively requires every nonzero operand-component pair to remain nonzero at the working scale; it can reject a physically finite result rather than silently lose a contribution.
