# examples/relaxation/uf6_collisions.m

`uf6_collisions()` illustrates how exchange into a quadrupole-relaxing state changes the fluorine line shape in a reduced UF6 model. It is a representative 19F–235U pair in each of two chemical species, not a simulation of the complete six-fluorine molecule or a prediction of gas-to-liquid experimental behaviour.

The symmetric species has zero uranium quadrupolar coupling; the distorted species has Cq=400 MHz and zero asymmetry. Both pairs have J=213 Hz, entered only once per pair. At 0.1 T, isotropic Redfield relaxation uses a fixed 10 fs correlation time in each species. The forward collision rate spans 1e10–9e13 s^-1, including 9e10 s^-1, while the reverse rate stays at 1e12 s^-1. Thus increasing collision frequency also increases the stationary distorted-state population; it does not shorten that state's 1 ps lifetime. The initial transverse coherence carries these stationary chemical populations, and detection sums both species without additional population weighting.

The calculation uses the complete spherical-tensor Liouville basis, full laboratory-frame relaxation, and `liquid(...,@slowpass,...,'labframe')`. Zero-track elimination is left off by default: the fast exchange scale must not be used to discard slowly developing J-coupled coherences in this demonstration. The spectrum is sampled directly at 0.5 Hz spacing across ±1200 Hz about the unshifted fluorine resonance. The waterfall shows frequency offset against the base-10 logarithm of the forward rate. Each absorptive trace is divided by its own maximum, so heights compare line shapes rather than absolute intensities.

The independent two-state exchange and rotational Redfield descriptions are phenomenological approximations. Collision trajectories, the other fluorines, and other relaxation mechanisms are omitted; the fastest rates are illustrative rather than calibrated liquid-state parameters. The example neither downloads inputs nor saves files automatically.
