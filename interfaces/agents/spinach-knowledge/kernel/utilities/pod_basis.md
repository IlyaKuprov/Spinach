# Weighted snapshot POD in normalised Euclidean coordinates

`pod_basis(blocks,weights,target_rank,options)` builds a fixed complex Euclidean basis from explicitly supplied snapshot matrices or covariance-equivalent factors. Weights multiply each block by their square root, without probability normalisation. Zero-weight blocks are validated but excluded from construction; supplied original dimensions still define the numerical-rank threshold and padded singular-value outputs.

Weighted data are represented in one common power-of-two frame before SVD, range products, QR and residual measurements. Rank depends on normalised singular values, not squared physical energy. Data normalisation that loses a nonzero component is unsupported and rejected explicitly. Physical singular values, absolute residual and squared energy may round to zero or infinity; `scale_exponent`, `singular_vals_scaled` and `data_norm_scaled` preserve the finite diagnostic frame. Relative residuals are measured directly on supplied data and remain scale independent.

Exact SVD allocates the weighted snapshot matrix and computes no unused right singular vectors. Randomised construction uses blockwise Gaussian range products, QR after every data/adjoint application, and SVD of compressed data: Halko, Martinsson and Tropp, DOI:10.1137/090771806, algorithms4.4 and5.1. Only retained basis modes are formed; Gaussian coefficients are released before compressed data. Data slices are bounded by sketch width; Gaussian coefficients and compressed arrays remain active-column by sketch-width allocations. Real active data retain real sketches, even with excluded complex blocks.

All option fields are required: method, oversampling, power_iters and seed. Exact SVD requires zero oversampling/power iterations; the seeded randomised method requires requested sketch size within original data dimensions. Integer inputs are double. Caller RNG is restored on success or error. Unsupported numerical rank raises an error rather than returning arbitrary nullspace directions.

POD minimises supplied snapshot reconstruction error, not reduced-trajectory or spectral error. Pre-truncated factors describe only the approximated data; discarded original dynamics are not recovered. Metrics other than the Euclidean inner product require caller-supplied whitening. See Benner, Gugercin and Willcox, DOI:10.1137/130932715, section3.3.
